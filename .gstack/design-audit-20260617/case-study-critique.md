# Earn with Yulu — Case Study Critique
**Date:** 2026-06-17
**Reviewer:** Design manager perspective
**Source:** https://www.figma.com/proto/VHlKxvi2mjquQZhTBtwIoS/Vaishnavi-Jawdekar_Portfolio?node-id=4-79

---

## Overall Verdict

The work is real, the outcome is strong, but the story is burying the best parts. A 96.4% fulfillment rate and a shipped MVP is the headline — right now it appears on slide 58 of 61.

---

## 1. Redundancy

### Context section is double the length it needs to be
- Slides 2, 3, 4 all cover the same ground: "Yulu serves gig workers, they deliver for Swiggy/Blinkit/Zomato, and they have idle time between shifts."
- Slide 4 (3-photo delivery flow) explains the client's business model to a hiring manager. Cut it.
- **Fix:** One tight context slide.

### Mission statement + benefits = same slide
- Slide 5: "maximize income with extra gigs"
- Slide 6: "Increased Earnings + Flexibility"
- Same idea, different fonts.
- **Fix:** Merge or cut one.

### Research insights appear twice
- Slide 16 (user quotes — want to earn more) and Slide 17 (motivations: earnings, distance, locations) make the same point.
- Quotes are evidence. Motivations are the synthesis.
- **Fix:** One slide — synthesis headline + supporting quotes below.

### Two "Video prototype / Home" slides with no framing
- Slides ~25 and ~40 both labelled "Video prototype / Home" showing different iterations.
- Reads as duplicate to an outside reviewer.
- **Fix:** Frame as V1 → V2 with one sentence explaining what changed and why.

### Two decision framework slides with inconsistent formats
- Slide 21: MoSCoW prioritisation
- Slide 35: In-app map pros/cons
- Different visual formats, different sections, feel disconnected.
- **Fix:** Standardize format or frame explicitly as "Key decisions we made."

---

## 2. Structure — Narrative Arc is Backwards

~18 slides before the first actual screen. ~30% of the deck is setup.

**Current structure:**
> Context → Context → Context → Context → Mission → Benefits → Problems → MVP decision → Research → Research → Design section break → Brainstorming → User journey → Prioritisation → Home iterations → Video prototype...

**Better structure:**
> Hook (96.4% fulfillment, shipped MVP) → Problem in 2 slides → What I designed (bulk) → Key decisions with rationale → Results

Lead with the number. "This shipped. Month 2: 96.4% order fulfillment." Then explain how you got there.

---

## 3. Color System — 7 Backgrounds, No Logic

Backgrounds used across content slides:
- Black (section breaks — consistent, good)
- White
- Cream/off-white
- Light purple
- Light green
- Light blue
- Light pink

No rule governing when each is used. Each slide designed in isolation.

Inline text accent colors also fragmented: orange (slide 5), green (slides 6/10/17), blue (slides 11/16), red (slide 8).

**Fix:** Pick 2–3 backgrounds total. Black for section breaks. One light neutral for content (white OR cream, not both). One accent background max for key insights.

| 6 | Emoji icons (💸 👑) read as informal in a portfolio case study | Replace with clean icons or remove |

## 5. What's Genuinely Strong

- Month 2 metrics slide: clean, clear, deltas from Month 1 visible (79.8% acceptance ↑, 96.4% fulfillment ↑, 3.6% cancellation ↓)
- Section break slides: consistent black, good pacing
- Product screens themselves: clean, focused, good info hierarchy on Order details (₹35, pickup, drop, Reject/Accept)
- Research method: shadowing + interviewing real delivery partners is solid framing
- Cluster-based delivery assignment decision: smart design thinking, just needs more prominence

---

## 6. Quick Wins (Ranked by Impact)

1. **Move Month 2 metrics to slide 2 or 3.** Open with the outcome.
2. **Cut slide 6 and one duplicate Home prototype slide.** Slide 4 (field photos) earns its place — fieldwork proof + pacing relief in a dense deck.
3. **Merge slides 16 + 17 into one research synthesis slide.**
4. **Pick one content background (white or cream) and apply consistently.**
5. **Frame V1 vs V2 explicitly on Home screen iteration.** One sentence: what changed and why.
