# Cal Case Study — Design Spec

## Overview

A case study section for Vaishnavi's portfolio about Cal, a personal calorie tracking iOS app she vibe-coded from scratch. The case study targets hiring teams and tells two parallel stories simultaneously: the product thinking behind Cal, and the personal experience of building a first app with no coding background.

## Narrative Structure

**Approach: Parallel Narrative (Option B)**

Two columns run side by side with a thin vertical divider. Left column = product decisions. Right column = what was actually happening personally. Three paired beats, each with a screenshot on the left and a personal note on the right. The columns converge in a full-width closing block.

---

## Section 1 — Header

**App name:** Cal (large type)

**Tagline:**
> "A calorie counter I built for myself because every other one made me feel bad."

**Label (subtle, below tagline):** `Side Project · Vibe Coded`

No metadata chips, no tags, no other clutter.

---

## Section 2 — Parallel Body

Two columns with a thin vertical divider. Left header: `The Product`. Right header: `What Was Actually Happening`.

### Beat 1 — The Why

**Left (Product):**
> "Every calorie app I tried had a goal. A deficit. A warning. I didn't want to be managed. I wanted to remember what I ate."

**Right (Personal):**
> "I had never written a line of code. I opened Claude and typed: *build me a calorie tracking app.*"

*(No screenshot for this beat — text only, sets the stage.)*

### Beat 2 — The Decisions

**Left (Product):**
Screenshot: Add Food screen.

Copy: Cal uses Indian portion units — katori, vati — because that's how I actually think about food. There's no calorie goal, no streak, no warning if you go over. The empty state reads "You haven't eaten yet :)" — not a nudge, just a fact.

**Right (Personal):**
> "I didn't realise I was making design decisions. I thought I was just telling it what I wanted."

### Beat 3 — The Moment

**Left (Product):**
Screenshot: Today view with a logged meal.

Copy: Cal uses the Gemini API to look up calories automatically. Type a food name and quantity, it returns a number. No database to maintain, no manual lookup.

**Right (Personal):**
> "The first time the API worked — *1 katori upma → 180 kcal* — I actually screamed. That was the moment I understood what building feels like."

---

## Section 3 — Closing (Full Width)

The two threads converge here. No columns.

**Primary paragraph:**
> "Cal is just for me. It lives on my phone, it knows my units, it doesn't judge me. Building it taught me that good product thinking and good code come from the same place — knowing exactly who you're designing for."

**Kicker line (quieter, smaller type):**
> "First API integration. First shipped app. Won't be the last."

No CTA, no link, no "view prototype" button.

## Visual Notes

- Screenshots needed: Add Food screen, Today view (with at least one logged meal)
- Portfolio aesthetic applies: `#F7F7F5` bg, `#0A0A0A` ink, `#4B4EFC` accent, Satoshi + DM Serif Display
- Divider between columns: subtle, thin — same separator treatment as the rest of the portfolio
- Column width: roughly 50/50, with screenshots sitting inside the left column at natural mobile proportions
- The closing block should feel like the two columns exhale into one — no hard visual break, just the divider ending

## Placement

Standalone section in the portfolio. Not inside the work grid. Positioned after the Selected Work section and before the About section. Acts as a bridge — "here's my design work, and here's proof I can build."
